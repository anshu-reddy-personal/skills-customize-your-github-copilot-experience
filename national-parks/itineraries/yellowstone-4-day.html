This itinerary covers the four major regions of Yellowstone in a logical loop from the South Entrance (Jackson Hole airport). You could also enter from the West or North entrances — the loop is the same. Reserve lodging or campsites 6+ months in advance for summer visits.

1
Day 1: Upper Geyser Basin & Old Faithful
Arrive via South Entrance → Old Faithful area
  • 7:00 AM
    Enter park at South Entrance; stop at Grant Village for breakfast
  • 9:00 AM
    Grand Prismatic Spring — Walk the boardwalk (free). Then hike the 1.6-mile Overlook Trail for the famous aerial rainbow view. Best in morning light.
  • 11:00 AM
    Old Faithful — Check the eruption prediction board at the Visitor Education Center (within ±10 min). Walk the boardwalk to see eruptions from multiple angles. Average interval ~90 minutes.
  • 12:30 PM
    Lunch at Old Faithful Inn dining room (make a reservation) or Old Faithful Lodge cafeteria
  • 2:00 PM
    Biscuit Basin & Black Sand Basin — Two underrated geyser basins just 2 miles north of Old Faithful with vivid blue pools and rainbow-colored runoff channels
  • 4:00 PM
    Check in at your lodging (Old Faithful area recommended for Day 1)
  • 6:00 PM
    Return to Old Faithful for the golden-hour eruption — the warm light is spectacular for photography
  • 8:30 PM
    Ranger-led evening program at the Old Faithful Amphitheater (summer only — check the park schedule)
💡 Pro tip: Check the Old Faithful eruption prediction at the visitor center as soon as you arrive. Plan the rest of your morning around the predicted eruption times to avoid waiting.
2
Day 2: Grand Canyon of Yellowstone & Hayden Valley
Central Yellowstone loop
  • 6:00 AM
    Hayden Valley wildlife patrol — Drive the valley slowly at dawn for bison, grizzly bears, coyotes, and trumpeter swans. Binoculars essential.
  • 8:30 AM
    Breakfast at Canyon Village dining room
  • 9:30 AM
    Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone — Drive the North Rim and South Rim roads. Must-see viewpoints: Artist Point (South Rim), Lookout Point, and Inspiration Point. The Lower Falls plunges 308 ft.
  • 11:30 AM
    Short hike down the Uncle Tom's Trail (328 steel steps) for a view from the base of the Lower Falls
  • 1:00 PM
    Picnic lunch at Cascade Meadow picnic area
  • 2:30 PM
    Mud Volcano area — Bizarre and bubbling mud pots, fumaroles, and acid pools. The Churning Caldron and Dragon's Mouth Spring are highlights.
  • 4:00 PM
    Drive north through Hayden Valley (afternoon is good for bison)
  • 6:00 PM
    Drive to Norris Geyser Basin for late afternoon — Norris is the hottest and most dynamic geyser basin in the park. Steamboat Geyser is the world's tallest active geyser.
3
Day 3: Lamar Valley — Wildlife Capital of the US
Northeast corner of the park
  • 5:30 AM
    Lamar Valley at dawn — This is the single best wildlife-viewing experience in Yellowstone. Wolves, grizzlies, bison, pronghorn, and eagles are routinely seen. Pull over at every turnout with spotting scopes set up.
  • 8:00 AM
    Breakfast at Roosevelt Lodge (charming 1920s-era log cabin lodge)
  • 9:30 AM
    Petrified Tree — Short walk to a 50-million-year-old standing fossilized redwood tree preserved by volcanic ash
  • 10:30 AM
    Tower Fall — 132-ft waterfall at the edge of a canyon; short 0.5-mile hike to viewpoint
  • 12:00 PM
    Picnic lunch at Slough Creek picnic area
  • 1:30 PM
    Optional: Hike the Slough Creek Trail for 2–4 miles into wolf and bear territory (bear spray required)
  • 4:00 PM
    Return through Lamar Valley for the afternoon wildlife window. Wolves are frequently active 1–3 hours before sunset.
  • 7:00 PM
    Dinner at Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel (drive north; ~45 minutes from Lamar Valley)
💡 Wolf watching: Join a Yellowstone Association wolf-watching tour or talk to volunteer wolf watchers in the valley — they have spotting scopes and decades of knowledge.
4
Day 4: Mammoth Hot Springs & South to Jackson Hole
North park → South Entrance → Grand Teton optional
  • 7:00 AM
    Mammoth Hot Springs Terraces — The travertine terraces change appearance daily as mineral-depositing springs shift. Walk the Upper and Lower Terraces boardwalk (1.75 miles). Elk frequently wander through the hotel grounds.
  • 9:00 AM
    Breakfast at Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel dining room
  • 10:00 AM
    Drive south on the Grand Loop Road through Norris, down through Gibbon Canyon, stopping at Gibbon Falls
  • 12:00 PM
    Final pass through the Midway Geyser Basin for last look at Grand Prismatic in midday light (the steam is more visible now)
  • 1:30 PM
    Lunch at Firehole Canyon pullout picnic area
  • 2:30 PM
    Exit via South Entrance; stop at the John D. Rockefeller Jr. Parkway viewpoint for a panorama of the Tetons
  • 4:00 PM
    Optional: Continue into Grand Teton NP for the iconic Snake River Overlook shot before driving to Jackson

🛎️ Practical Information

Lodging

Book Old Faithful Inn, Canyon Lodge, or Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel through Yellowstone Forever 6–12 months ahead. Budget option: Madison, Norris, or Canyon campgrounds via Recreation.gov.

Getting Around

A car is essential — there is no shuttle system in Yellowstone. The Grand Loop Road (142 miles) connects all major areas. Allow extra time for wildlife jams (bison on the road are common and the law requires you to wait).

Cell Service

Spotty at best. Download offline maps before entering. WiFi available at visitor centers and some lodges.

Bear Safety

Carry bear spray at all times on trails. Maintain 100-yard distance from bears and wolves. Store all food and scented items in designated bear boxes.

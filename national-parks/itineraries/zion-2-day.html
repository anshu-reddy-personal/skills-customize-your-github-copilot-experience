Trip Overview

Two full days covering Zion's greatest hits. Private vehicles are not permitted on Zion Canyon Scenic Drive — park at the visitor center and use the free shuttle. Angels Landing requires an advance permit via Recreation.gov (seasonal lottery, highly competitive). Book months in advance.

⚠️ Flash flood risk in The Narrows: Check the NPS flood forecast the morning of your hike. If a flash flood watch or warning is issued, do NOT enter The Narrows — floods arrive without warning and are fatal.
1
Day 1: The Narrows & Angels Landing
The two bucket-list hikes
  • 6:00 AM
    The Narrows — Take the shuttle to Temple of Sinawava (Stop 9). Walk the 1.1-mile paved Riverside Walk to where it ends at the river. Wade upstream into the slot canyon. Rent dry shoes/wetsuit from Zion Outfitter in Springdale for spring/fall.
  • 9:30 AM
    Return shuttle to Zion Lodge for breakfast at Red Rock Grill
  • 11:00 AM
    Angels Landing (permit required) — Shuttle to The Grotto (Stop 6). The 5.4-mile round-trip climbs 1,488 ft. The final half-mile uses chains fixed to the cliff. Spectacular summit views of the full canyon. Allow 4–5 hours.
  • 4:00 PM
    Return to visitor center. Rest and recharge at your lodging in Springdale.
  • 6:30 PM
    Sunset from Canyon Junction Bridge — the canyon walls glow orange at golden hour from this iconic spot on the park road
💡 Do The Narrows in the morning and Angels Landing in late morning when the summit chains section is less crowded. Or reverse the order if your permit time is morning.
2
Day 2: Emerald Pools, Canyon Overlook & Scenic Drive
Waterfalls, overlooks, and the Mount Carmel Highway
  • 7:00 AM
    Emerald Pools — Shuttle to Zion Lodge (Stop 5). Hike to Lower, Middle, and Upper Emerald Pools (3 miles round-trip). Hanging gardens, waterfalls, and cliff-backed pools. Do it early before the trail gets crowded.
  • 9:30 AM
    Breakfast at Zion Lodge or packed food at the patio picnic tables
  • 10:30 AM
    Weeping Rock (Stop 7) — A short 0.4-mile paved trail to a dripping alcove where water seeps through the canyon sandstone, creating hanging gardens of ferns and wildflowers
  • 11:30 AM
    Exit the canyon via the Zion-Mount Carmel Highway. Drive east through the long Zion-Mount Carmel Tunnel (RVs and large vehicles need escort — check hours) to the east side of the park.
  • 12:00 PM
    Canyon Overlook Trail — Start at the parking area just east of the tunnel. This 1-mile trail is the best "bang for effort" view hike in the park — canyon panoramas with minimal climbing.
  • 1:30 PM
    Lunch at East Zion area (bring your own) or drive to Mount Carmel Junction for food
  • 3:00 PM
    Drive to Bryce Canyon (1 hour) or Las Vegas (2.5 hours) for your next destination. Or add a 3rd day in Zion for Kolob Canyons (45 minutes northwest) — completely different canyon scenery with far fewer visitors.

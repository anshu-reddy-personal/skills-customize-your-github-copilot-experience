Trip Overview

This 3-day plan balances iconic rim views with inner-canyon hiking. The itinerary is written for spring or fall visits (March–May or September–November). If visiting in summer, start all hikes by 5 AM and be back at the rim by 10 AM to avoid dangerous heat.

⚠️ Heat kills in the Grand Canyon. Inner-canyon temperatures regularly exceed 110°F in summer. Never hike from rim to river and back in a single summer day. The canyon is twice as hard to hike out of as in — plan accordingly.
1
Day 1: Arrival, Rim Trail & Sunset
South Rim orientation
  • Morning
    Arrive at Grand Canyon Village. Check in at Bright Angel Lodge or El Tovar Hotel. Both are steps from the rim.
  • 11:00 AM
    Mather Point — Your first canyon view. Take time to let it sink in. Walk east along the Rim Trail to Yavapai Point and the Geology Museum (free).
  • 12:30 PM
    Lunch at Bright Angel Restaurant or El Tovar Dining Room (reservations required for El Tovar)
  • 2:00 PM
    Rim Trail west section — Walk from the Village to Trailview Overlook for a top-down view of the Bright Angel Trail switchbacks
  • 4:00 PM
    Ride the free Village Route shuttle to Hermits Rest Road overlooks (Pima Point, Mohave Point). These west-facing overlooks are best in the late afternoon.
  • 6:30 PM
    Sunset at Hopi Point — The best sunset viewpoint on the South Rim. Arrive 45 minutes early in peak season to secure a spot. California condors often soar past at sunset.
2
Day 2: Inner Canyon Hike — Bright Angel Trail
The classic inner canyon experience
  • 5:30 AM
    Start hiking Bright Angel Trail — Begin before sunrise. The trail descends 4,460 feet over 9.5 miles one-way to the Colorado River.
  • 7:00 AM
    Reach the 1.5-mile Resthouse (first water and emergency phone). This is the turnaround point for casual hikers in summer. In spring/fall, continue to 3-Mile Resthouse.
  • 9:00 AM
    3-Mile Resthouse — Water, shade, and emergency phone. Spectacular views into the inner canyon. Strong hikers (spring/fall only) can continue 1.5 miles to Indian Garden (Havasupai Gardens) oasis.
  • 10:30 AM
    Turn around and climb out. The return trip feels twice as long. Drink before you're thirsty; eat salty snacks.
  • 1:00 PM
    Return to the rim. Rest, shower, rehydrate. The Canyon is done with you for the day.
  • 3:00 PM
    Yavapai Geology Museum (free) — Excellent exhibits explaining the canyon's 2-billion-year geological history.
  • 5:00 PM
    Sunset from Mather Point or Pipe Creek Vista
💡 Bring at least 3 liters of water per person for a 3-Mile Resthouse round-trip. Electrolyte packets are critical — plain water won't prevent hyponatremia (dangerous low sodium from drinking too much water without electrolytes).
3
Day 3: Desert View Drive & South Kaibab Overview
Eastern rim and departure
  • 5:45 AM
    Sunrise at Mather Point or Desert View — A different light than sunset — the rim glows pink before the canyon fills with color
  • 7:00 AM
    Breakfast at Bright Angel Restaurant
  • 8:30 AM
    South Kaibab Trailhead — Hike 1.5 miles down to Ooh Aah Point for the best panoramic view of the canyon without going too deep. No shade and no water — bring plenty of both.
  • 10:30 AM
    Drive east along Desert View Drive (26 miles of overlooks). Stop at Grandview Point, Moran Point, Lipan Point, and Navajo Point.
  • 12:30 PM
    Desert View Watchtower — Mary Colter's 1932 stone tower at the canyon's eastern edge. Climb to the top for 360° views. Good lunch stop at the nearby snack bar.
  • 2:30 PM
    Drive east to Cameron for lunch at Cameron Trading Post (authentic Navajo food and crafts), then continue to your next destination or fly home from Flagstaff (1 hour) or Phoenix (3.5 hours).

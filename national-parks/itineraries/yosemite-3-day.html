Trip Overview

Important: A timed-entry reservation is required May–September to drive into Yosemite Valley. Reservations open 2 months in advance at Recreation.gov and sell out within minutes. Once in the Valley, use the free YARTS shuttle system to reach trailheads.

💡 For the best photos and fewest crowds, arrive at major viewpoints at sunrise or after 5 PM. Midday in Yosemite Valley in July is extremely crowded.
1
Day 1: Yosemite Valley Icons
Tunnel View → Valley floor → Yosemite Falls
  • 6:00 AM
    Tunnel View — Pull over at the east end of the Wawona Tunnel for the classic panorama of El Capitan, Half Dome, and Bridalveil Fall. Best in morning light with mist in the valley.
  • 7:00 AM
    Drive to Valley Visitor Center; park once for the day and use the free shuttle
  • 7:30 AM
    El Cap Meadow — Walk to the flat meadow below El Capitan and look up at the 3,000-ft granite wall. Watch for climbers — binoculars help.
  • 9:00 AM
    Breakfast at Yosemite Valley Lodge or Village Grill (quick service)
  • 10:00 AM
    Yosemite Falls Trail — Hike to Lower Yosemite Falls (easy, 1 mile) or continue all the way to the top (7.2 miles, strenuous). The lower falls alone are worth the short walk.
  • 12:30 PM
    Picnic lunch at Cook's Meadow with views of Half Dome and Sentinel Rock
  • 2:00 PM
    Mirror Lake Loop — 5-mile loop through forest to a glacial lake with stunning Half Dome reflections (best reflections in spring/early summer)
  • 5:00 PM
    Valley View — Merced River pullout near the western valley entrance for the classic reflection shot of El Capitan
  • 7:00 PM
    Dinner at The Ahwahnee dining room (reservations required — book months ahead) or Degnan's Kitchen
2
Day 2: High Country — Bear Lake and Glacier Point
Alpine lakes → panoramic overlook
  • 7:00 AM
    Bear Lake Trailhead — Drive to the Bear Lake area. Hike the 3.6-mile loop through Nymph Lake and Dream Lake to Emerald Lake, each with postcard-perfect mountain reflections.
  • 10:30 AM
    Drive to Glacier Point Road (open June–November). Stop at Sentinel Dome trailhead — a 2.2-mile hike to a bare granite dome with 360° views.
  • 1:00 PM
    Glacier Point — The most spectacular overlook in Yosemite, 3,200 ft above the valley floor. Direct view of Half Dome, Nevada Fall, and Vernal Fall. Visit the small museum. Arrive early afternoon before tour buses crowd the parking lot.
  • 2:30 PM
    Picnic at Glacier Point (arrive with food — no dining service here)
  • 4:00 PM
    Drive back to the Valley; stop at Taft Point if time allows (1-mile walk to a frightening cliff edge with railing-free drops of 3,500 ft)
  • 6:30 PM
    Sunset from Sentinel Bridge — Half Dome reflected in the Merced River at golden hour. One of the most photographed spots in national park history.
3
Day 3: Mariposa Grove Giant Sequoias
South of the Valley → departure
  • 7:30 AM
    Drive south to the Mariposa Grove parking area (6 miles from South Entrance). Take the free shuttle to the grove trailhead (required when lot is full, typically after 9 AM)
  • 8:30 AM
    Big Trees Loop — 2-mile flat walk among the ancient sequoias. The Grizzly Giant (1,800+ years old) and California Tunnel Tree are must-sees. The Cathedral Group is stunning.
  • 10:30 AM
    Drive or shuttle to Wawona Hotel area for brunch
  • 12:00 PM
    Optional: Walk through the Wawona Meadow for deer watching (2.3-mile flat loop)
  • 1:30 PM
    Exit via South Entrance toward Fresno (2 hrs) or San Francisco (3.5 hrs via CA-140 through Merced)
💡 Bonus day? If you have a 4th day, spend it at Tuolumne Meadows (open July–October). It's high-elevation, crowd-free compared to the Valley, and stunningly beautiful. The Cathedral Lakes hike (8 miles) is a highlight.

🎒 The Universal Day-Hike Kit

Pack these on every single day hike, regardless of distance or season.

💡 The Ten Essentials — originally published by The Mountaineers in 1974 — remain the gold standard for wilderness preparedness. Every item below falls into one of the ten essential systems.

Navigation

  • Downloaded offline map (Gaia GPS or AllTrails)
  • Paper trail map (free at most park visitor centers)
  • Compass (even if you use a phone)
  • Park permit / reservation confirmation

Sun Protection

  • Sunscreen SPF 30+ (apply before leaving)
  • Lip balm with SPF
  • Sunglasses (UV400)
  • Wide-brim hat or buff

Insulation

  • Moisture-wicking base layer
  • Mid-layer (fleece or down jacket)
  • Waterproof / wind-resistant shell
  • Extra socks (wool recommended)
  • Gloves or liner gloves (for alpine parks)
  • Beanie / warm hat (above treeline)

Illumination

  • Headlamp (not a phone torch)
  • Extra batteries or charged power bank

First Aid

  • Blister treatment (moleskin / Leukotape)
  • Antiseptic wipes and antibiotic ointment
  • Bandages and medical tape
  • Ibuprofen / acetaminophen
  • Antihistamine (for bee stings / allergies)
  • Personal medications + prescription copy
  • Emergency whistle
  • Emergency mylar blanket

Fire (emergency use only)

  • Waterproof matches or lighter
  • Fire starter (tinder tabs or fatwood)

Repair Tools & Knife

  • Multi-tool or pocket knife
  • Duct tape (wrapped around a water bottle saves space)
  • Trekking poles (optional; highly recommended for steep trails)

Nutrition

  • High-energy snacks (trail mix, bars, jerky)
  • Extra day's food beyond your planned amount
  • Lunch or packed meal
  • Electrolyte packets (critical in desert parks)

Hydration

  • 2L water minimum per person per day (desert: 4L)
  • Water filter or purification tablets
  • Hydration bladder or sturdy water bottles

Emergency Shelter

  • Emergency bivy or space blanket
  • Rain poncho (doubles as emergency shelter)

🏕️ Overnight & Backpacking Additions

  • Backcountry permit (book via Recreation.gov)
  • Bear canister or hang system (required in many parks)
  • Tent or tarp with stakes
  • Sleeping bag rated for expected lows + 10°F
  • Sleeping pad (insulated for cold ground)
  • Backpacking stove + fuel canister
  • Cookpot, spork, and camp mug
  • Freeze-dried or lightweight meals
  • Water filter (mandatory for backcountry)
  • Trowel (cat holes 6–8 in deep, 200 ft from water)
  • Waste bags and pack-out kit
  • Biodegradable soap (use 200 ft from water)
  • Stuff sacks for food smell-proofing
  • Power bank for GPS / phone
  • Satellite communicator (e.g., Garmin inReach) — strongly recommended

🏔️ Park-Specific Add-Ons

🌵 Desert Parks
(Arches, Joshua Tree, Grand Canyon inner canyon)

  • 4L+ water per person per day
  • Electrolytes (vital — plain water isn't enough)
  • Sun hoody or UPF shirt
  • Cooling towel or misting bottle
  • Gaiters (sand in shoes is miserable)
  • Start hiking before 7 AM in summer

🧊 Alpine / Glacier Parks
(Glacier, Grand Teton, Rocky Mountain)

  • Crampons or Microspikes (shoulder seasons)
  • Ice axe (technical routes)
  • Bear spray (grizzly country)
  • Avalanche awareness (winter)
  • Layering system — weather changes fast

🌊 Coastal / Rainforest Parks
(Acadia, Olympic)

  • Waterproof jacket (rain every day)
  • Waterproof boots or gaiters
  • Dry bags for electronics
  • Tide chart (for coastal hikes)
  • Fleece or wool layers (damp cold)

🌊 Slot Canyon Hikes
(Zion Narrows, Fiery Furnace)

  • Wetsuit or drysuit (Narrows in spring/fall)
  • Neoprene socks and canyoneering boots
  • Trekking poles for river crossing
  • Waterproof pack or dry bag
  • Flash flood weather check before entry

🐻 Wildlife Safety Essentials

⚠️ Bear country: Carry and know how to use bear spray in grizzly habitat (Yellowstone, Grand Teton, Glacier, Denali). Practice deploying it before you hike.
  • Bear spray (grizzly parks — not optional)
  • Bear canister or bear hang system (backcountry)
  • Snake bite first aid knowledge (not a kit — kits don't work; evacuate)
  • Know park-specific wildlife distances (100 yd from bears/wolves, 25 yd from others)
